# Final Video Production Guidelines – LockerEasy  
*Comprehensive instructions for the final 3-Stage documentary-style video.*

---

## 1. **Objective**

The purpose of this video is to present a complete, structured, and honest overview of the development process across **Stage 1, Stage 2, and Stage 3** of the LockerEasy project.  
The video should focus on:

- The evolution of the team.  
- Challenges encountered at different stages.  
- Key decisions, setbacks, and learning moments.  
- How the team matured and prepared for the next programming phase.

This is not a technical demonstration; it is a **project narrative and reflective summary**.

---

## 2. **Narrative Concept**

The video should be structured as a **three-chapter documentary**, narrated entirely by the presenter (Nico).  
The chapters are:

1. **Stage 1 – Initial Planning and Foundations**  
2. **Stage 2 – Growth, Design Expansion, and Increased Complexity**  
3. **Stage 3 – Difficulties, Re-evaluation, and Team Consolidation**

Audio contributions from team members will be used as supporting testimonies—**voice-only**, without on-camera appearances.

Clips from the previous Stage 1 and Stage 2 videos may be inserted to reinforce context.

---

## 3. **Tone & Communication Style**

- Serious, calm, and professional.  
- Clear and reflective narration.  
- First-person perspective from the presenter, but avoiding exaggerated personal expressions.  
- No unnecessary technical detail; only short references when relevant.  
- The emotional arc should convey:  
  **initial motivation → increasing complexity → confronting difficulties → learning → confidence moving forward**.

---

## 4. **Visual Style**

### Presenter

- The presenter is the **only person visible on camera** throughout the entire video.  
- Use a stable **medium shot** (torso to head).  
- Background should be neutral, organized, and free of visual noise.  
- Lighting must be soft, even, and non-distracting.

### Supporting Visuals

Use them to complement narration:

- Clips from previous videos (Stage 1 & 2).  
- Screenshots of the backlog, UML diagrams, repository, or planning documents.  
- Short B-roll: workspace, notes, small animations, etc.  
- Graphic panels for team members’ voice-only interventions.

---

## 5. **Use of Teammates’ Voices**

Team members will appear **only through short audio recordings** (10–20 seconds).  
Their purpose is to provide:

- Personal reflections  
- Confirmation of shared difficulties  
- Brief comments on teamwork or project stages  

They should not narrate events; they only reinforce key moments of the story.

---

## 6. **Recommended Video Structure**

### **1. Introduction (50–60 seconds)**

- Present the project and explain that the video summarizes all three stages.  
- Set expectations: this is a structured analysis of the full development process.  
- Insert 1–2 short clips or images from early development.

---

### **2. Stage 1 – Foundations (45–60 seconds)**

- Explain initial goals and team formation.  
- Describe the early planning process and first deliverables.  
- Use short archival footage or related visuals.

---

### **3. Stage 2 – Growth and Design (60–80 seconds)**

- Describe the expansion of requirements.  
- Show how UML modeling and design choices became more complex.  
- Insert 1–2 teammate audio clips reflecting on challenges or design decisions.

---

### **4. Stage 3 – Reassessment and Team Maturity (2–3 minutes)**

This section represents the core of the video.

Discuss:

- Time pressure and delayed progress  
- Team difficulties and organizational issues  
- The need to restructure priorities  
- What decisions were made to regain direction  
- How the team improved communication and collaboration  
- Lessons learned and how they apply to future work  

Integrate several short teammate audio testimonies to highlight emotional and collaborative aspects.

---

### **5. Conclusion (30–40 seconds)**

- Summarize the main insights from the three stages.  
- Affirm the team’s readiness to enter the programming stage.  
- Close with a serious and confident message about the project’s next steps.  
- Display the LockerEasy logo.

---

## 7. **Audio & Music Guidelines**

- Use soft, unobtrusive background music.  
- Reduce music volume significantly during dialogue.  
- Ensure voice recordings are clean, without echo or background noise.  
- Maintain consistent audio levels throughout all segments.

---

## 8. **Editing Guidelines**

- Use clean, simple cuts—avoid flashy transitions.  
- Insert chapter titles (“Stage 1”, “Stage 2”, “Stage 3”) with minimalistic style.  
- Include subtle subtitles for team voice clips if necessary.  
- Keep visuals cohesive: consistent color palette (black, white, blue tones).

---

## 9. **Avoid the Following**

- Overly technical explanations (the video must remain narrative-focused).  
- Long segments without visual variation.  
- Excessively personal or informal expressions.  
- Loud music or inconsistent audio transitions.  
- Redundant explanations or repeated statements.

---

## 10. **Final Notes**

- The video should feel like the **closing chapter of a complete project journey**.  
- The central message is:  
  **“Beyond technical progress, our team strengthened its process, communication, and readiness for development.”**  
- Recommended final video length: **5 to 7 minutes**.  
- Prioritize clarity, professionalism, and narrative cohesion.
